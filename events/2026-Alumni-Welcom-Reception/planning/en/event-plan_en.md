## 1. Positioning and Goals

This event is hosted by the **Yale University Shanghai Alumni Association** and is intended for alumni in Shanghai and nearby regions, admitted incoming students and newly enrolled students, and industry and academic partners interested in AI adoption and interdisciplinary collaboration.

### Core Goals

- **Strengthen the alumni brand**: reinforce the public identity of the Yale Shanghai alumni network so its local influence is easier to understand, recognize, and connect with.
- **Help incoming students make direction choices**: bring together alumni at different stages of development and local companies to provide dense information and connections that support choices around training paths, industry focus, and personal positioning.
- **Create open dialogue and interdisciplinary collaboration**: shift from one-way life-story sharing toward Fishbowl and roundtable formats that reflect Yale's interdisciplinary character across the humanities and sciences.
- **Make AI tangible on site**: organize a light co-creation / mini hackathon based on **OpenClaw**, lowering environment-setup barriers so even participants without technical backgrounds can join and produce demos.

### Co-Organizing Partner

**MuShanghai**: The Mu team specializes in international pop-up city events across Southeast Asia, North America, and South America. MuShanghai is its Shanghai local initiative. One of the Shanghai project initiators, Frank Ji, is a Yale Class of 2020 PhD graduate in the Biological & Biomedical Sciences program, Computational Biology & Bioinformatics track.

## 2. Scale, Venue, and Audience

- **Scale**: 150-200 people
- **Time**: April 18 (or 19) or April 25 (or 26)
- **Venue**: Lujiazui Digital Intelligence Port (tentative)
- **Audience mix**: alumni in Shanghai (business owners, executives, researchers, younger alumni), incoming students, and representatives from peer schools and partner organizations. A rough mix is local supporting alumni, visiting alumni and friends/family, and incoming students at about **1 : 3 : 6**.
- **Audience value proposition**: many local business owners have genuine interest in buying or partnering around foundation-model and AI services, but lack a trusted on-site understanding of what that looks like in practice. This event can turn abstract concepts into experiential understanding.

## 3. Program Structure (Recommended Framework)

### 1. Opening and Host / Organizer Introduction (10-15 min)

- Yale alumni association: positioning, alumni ecosystem, and Yale's interdisciplinary strengths
- MuShanghai: open co-creation mechanism and event framework

### 2. Intergenerational Alumni Sharing (45-60 min)

- More established alumni: perspectives from investment, company management, and platform ecosystems
- Rapid-growth stage alumni: academic and industry frontiers and paths to translation
- Younger alumni: cross-disciplinary work in art, architecture, culture, and AI collaboration

### 3. Open Discussion (Fishbowl / Open Panel, 45-60 min)

- Moderated discussion with audience members able to join the conversation on stage
- Themes: AI adoption, interdisciplinary collaboration, local opportunities in Shanghai, and pre-enrollment decisions

### 4. Light Hackathon / Co-Creation Block (1.5-2.5 h) - Based on OpenClaw

- Teams form on site to complete tasks that can produce a deliverable within two hours
- Productized OpenClaw standard offerings (such as **MaxClaw**) serve as the unified entry point and plugin-style capability layer, reducing deployment and environment friction
- Teams produce demos / prototypes and then present them briefly at the end
- A sofa discussion area will be reserved for attendees who are not interested in the hackathon and would rather talk freely

### 5. Two Maps (Core Outputs)

Participants will be guided into mixed teams of technical and non-technical alumni and incoming students to create interactive outputs with some aesthetic quality and meaningful complexity.

- **Alumni Journey Map**: helps incoming students understand where alumni with similar academic backgrounds came from, what they experienced at Yale, and where they went after graduation / why, with diversity emphasized
- **Local Guide Map**: created with guidance from current students, covering life, culture, arts walks, and practical resource lists for local living

### 6. Wrap-Up and Thanks (10 min)

## 5. Value for Alumni Organizations and Institutions

- **High-quality local reach**: the event brings together alumni-run companies and managers in Shanghai, creating a high-trust context for reaching potential AI buyers and collaborators. We would prioritize connections with alumni-led companies, organizations, institutions, and their portfolio ecosystems.
- **Visible AI capability**: OpenClaw-based light co-creation makes applied AI capabilities tangible on site and helps technical partners become easier to recognize within the AI application ecosystem.
- **Long-term alumni brand assets**: support and endorsement from alumni organizations and institutions can significantly strengthen the event's pull while helping establish a longer-term collaboration model between the alumni brand and the innovation ecosystem.

## 6. Support We Are Currently Seeking

- **On-site technical guidance**: alumni and partners with relatively strong hands-on AI tool experience who can join the volunteer flow and support event teams on site.
