## 1. Purpose

This document is the execution runbook for volunteer recruitment and on-site operations for the Yale Shanghai alumni event in late April 2026. It is written so the organizing team can act immediately even if the final date and venue are still being confirmed.

The event arc is:
- Opening and context-setting
- Intergenerational sharing
- Fishbowl discussion on how younger people face AI disruption and build toward the future
- OpenClaw co-creation block as the practical bridge from discussion to action

This plan covers:
- volunteer recruiting
- role assignment
- training and readiness
- day-of operations
- contingency handling

### Event Flow at a Glance

| Phase | What Attendees Experience | Volunteer Focus | Lead |
|---|---|---|---|
| Setup (T-180 to doors open) | - | Venue, tech, front-desk readiness | Ops/Tech Lead |
| Arrival | Warm welcome, smooth check-in | Check-in, badge flow, directing guests | People Lead |
| Opening + Sharing (55-75 min) | Context-setting, intergenerational stories | Time-keeping, mic, speaker flow | Program Lead |
| Fishbowl (45-60 min) | Open AI discussion, audience joins on stage | Participation flow, time gate, prep groups | Program Lead |
| Transition (~10 min) | Energy shift: discussion becomes action | Execute transition script, form groups | Program Lead + Ops/Tech Lead |
| Co-Creation (1.5-2.5 h) | Hands-on OpenClaw in teams, sofa chat area | Guide groups, troubleshoot, track outputs | Ops/Tech Lead |
| Wrap-up + Teardown | Demo showcase, thanks, departure | Collect outputs, close venue, clean up | Program Lead + Ops/Tech Lead |

### Volunteer Headcount Model

| Function | Headcount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Check-in and welcome | 2 | Peak load is arrival window |
| Stage and program support | 2 | Includes mic movement and speaker coordination |
| OpenClaw fixed guides | 3 | Each covers a cluster of groups |

## 4. Recruiting Plan
> **Arc focus:** Pre-event preparation.
> **If this fails:** The team arrives understaffed and guest experience suffers from the first minute.

### Recruiting Principles
- Recruit from Yale alumni only for volunteer roles.
- Use trusted non-alumni friends as guests or fishbowl voices, not as volunteers in this plan.
- Over-recruit the interest pool: target 18-20 prospects to land 12-15 confirmed volunteers.
- Each lead owns a recruiting quota; People Lead coordinates the master pipeline.

### Recruiting Timeline
| Time | Owner | Steps | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|---|
| This week | People Lead | Create master sheet with columns for function, lead, volunteer, recruiter, OpenClaw installed, status, backup | Working volunteer tracker |
| This week | All leads | Submit 8-12 prospects each and send wave-1 soft outreach | Initial prospect pool |
| T-5 weeks | People Lead | Review responses, tag people as interested / maybe / confirmed | Status dashboard |
| T-4 weeks | All leads | Run wave-2 outreach to fill role gaps | Gap-fill candidate list |
| T-3 weeks | People Lead + all leads | Match volunteers to roles and identify backups | Draft role roster |
| T-2 weeks | Event Lead | Confirm final role structure and arrival windows | Locked staffing model |
| T-1 week | People Lead | Freeze roster except backups | Final volunteer roster |

### Outreach Steps
1. Build the long list.
2. Segment prospects by role fit: guest-facing, stage, tech, logistics, backup.
3. Start with personal outreach from the most relevant lead.
4. Follow up within 3-4 days if no response.
5. Move undecided people to backup unless they confirm.
6. Do not assign day-of critical roles to volunteers who cannot attend briefing or rehearsal.

## 5. Volunteer Readiness Rules
> **Arc focus:** Pre-event preparation.
> **If this fails:** Volunteers enter live phases unsure of roles, while tech blockers spill into attendee time.

Every volunteer must complete the following before going on shift:

| Requirement | Applies To | Owner | Deadline | Why This Matters |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Role confirmed in writing | All volunteers | People Lead | T-7 days | Prevents day-of confusion about who does what |
| Joined main group chat | All volunteers | People Lead | T-7 days | Keeps last-mile updates from getting missed |
| Read role brief and run-of-show | All volunteers | Relevant Lead | T-5 days | Gives every person a shared picture of event flow |
| Knows escalation path | All volunteers | Relevant Lead | T-5 days | Shortens incident response when time is tight |
| Attended briefing | All volunteers | People Lead | T-5 to T-3 days | Aligns expectations before on-site pressure starts |
| Completed rehearsal / readiness check | Program + Ops/Tech teams and their volunteers | Relevant Lead | T-5 to T-3 days | Prevents cue and handoff errors during live phases |
| OpenClaw installed and tested | Fixed guides + rover | Ops/Tech Lead | T-7 to T-5 days | Guides can help groups immediately, no setup delay |

Hard gate: if a fixed guide or rover has not installed and tested OpenClaw by T-5 days, move them out of the tech role and replace them with backup.

## 8. Fishbowl to OpenClaw Transition Runbook
> **Arc focus:** Fishbowl to Co-Creation handoff.
> **If this fails:** Dead air grows, groups form slowly, and co-creation starts late.

This is the most protected operational moment of the event.

### Normal Flow

1. At 10 minutes before fishbowl end, Program Lead signals Ops/Tech Lead to take positions.
2. Fixed guides move visibly to their group zones.
3. Rover checks one last time that install and fallback demos are ready.
4. Moderator closes with a forward-motion script, discussion becomes action.
5. Facilitator directs attendees into 6-8 groups.
6. Fixed guides receive their group clusters immediately.
7. Rover handles the first blocked group, then keeps moving.

### Shortened Variant

If fishbowl runs late:
- Program Lead cuts the final audience round.
- Moderator uses shortened closing script.
- Group formation is limited to pre-defined clusters.
- Co-creation start may lose no more than 10 minutes.

## 9. Technical Fallback Plan
> **Arc focus:** Co-Creation block.
> **If this fails:** Attendees get stuck, and the promise of collaboration with visible outputs is broken.

| Mode | Trigger | Owner | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal mode | Wi-Fi and tools work for most groups | Ops/Tech Lead | Run full OpenClaw flow |
| Degraded mode | Some installs fail or throughput is slow | Rover | Shift blocked groups to shared-demo or guide-assisted mode |
| Fallback mode | Too many teams blocked or infrastructure unstable | Event Lead + Ops/Tech Lead | Run guided ideation / mapping exercise instead of full live tool use |

Important: fallback must still preserve the event promise of collaboration and visible outputs.

## 10. Day-of Command Sheet
> **Arc focus:** Live command across all phases.
> **If this fails:** Decisions stall, cross-team alignment breaks, and timing slips compound.

- Event Lead: final event calls, external coordination, and top-level escalation.
- People Lead: volunteer roster changes, check-in operations, and guest-flow issue handling.
- Program Lead: program timing changes, speaker flow, and moderator handoff decisions.
- Ops/Tech Lead: venue issue escalation, setup and teardown control, and tech mode switch.

Escalation order:
1. Function lead handles direct issue.
2. If unresolved in 3 minutes, escalate to Event Lead.
3. If agenda timing is affected, Program Lead updates all leads immediately.
